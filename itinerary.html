Itinerary

Seceda and the Odle · photo: Wolfgang Moroder / CC BY-SA 3.0

Five weeks, and the route is set: Sydney, Singapore and Vienna to get there (booked, with a climbing gym at each stop), the Dolomites and Arco for the heart of it, then Slovenia, a German split, and Kalymnos for the finale, flying home from Athens on 6 or 7 October.

The order still follows the calendar. Early September is the Dolomites' best fortnight, Arco is good all month, Slovenia's karst crags open their season as we arrive with fresh kits, and Kalymnos improves right up to its festival. Germany sits where it does because that is where the friends are, and Oktoberfest happens to be running when Sam passes through Munich.

Getting there: 1-4 September, booked

Tue 1 Sept
Jetstar Brisbane to Sydney, night with the Dulwich Hill crew.
Wed 2 Sept, early
Sydney Indoor Climbing Gym St Peters, ten minutes from Dulwich Hill and on the way to the airport, opens at 06:00 on Wednesdays (the only day it opens that early). Climb 6:30-9:30 on top rope (walk-in after a ten-minute belay check; leading needs a pre-arranged test, 02 9519 3325), about $80 for two with full hire, then the 12-minute drive to T1 for the 13:00 Scoot departure. The no-time-pressure version is Tuesday evening instead, open to 22:00.
Wed 2 Sept, evening
Land Changi 19:30 with cabin bags only (the checked bags ride through on the single Scoot ticket; SG Arrival Cards submitted from Sydney). Fifteen minutes to BFF Climb at Tampines Hub: day pass S$23.98, shoes S$5.45, and their on-demand pair belay verification (S$10.90) puts us on real top ropes until about 22:30. Back airside by 23:15 for the ~02:45 Vienna flight. If we land late, the fallback is Climb@T3 in Terminal 3's public basement.
Thu 3 Sept
Land Vienna ~09:00 with the whole day in hand. Two ways to the valley: if the Milan friend confirms, a Vienna day then Ryanair at 18:20 to Bergamo, the evening in Milan, and the morning Frecciarossa to Bolzano plus bus 350, reaching Ortisei mid-afternoon on the 4th. If Milan stays a maybe, the day train Vienna-Bolzano via Innsbruck (6.5-7.5 h, advance fares ~€35-45), a night in Bolzano, and the 09:00 bus up, which buys the only fully easy 4th. The night-train option died with the timetable: Vienna-Milan no longer runs.
Fri 4 Sept
Ortisei. Recovery day by design: guest pass, a flat Alpe di Siusi stroll if the legs ask for it, early night.

The Dolomites: 5-11 September

Val Gardena base first, then a fork on the 8th: the Cortina side (version A) or the rooms-only hut traverse (version B). The bus network covers everything below, and a hire car is a real option for version A only (Bolzano pickup on the 4th, Trento drop on the 11th, net ~€180-380 for two after bus savings): it buys back Val di Funes, dawn starts on the east side and weather agility, but it cannot follow the traverse. The fuller car comparison is on Decisions, and every hike and ferrata is compared side by side (effort, difficulty, crowds, access, photo-rich guide links) on Destinations.

Sat 5 Sept
Seceda ridgeline. Pre-book the lift slot (new for 2026); ride up, walk the rim, come down via Col Raiser to dodge the €74 return fare.
Sun 6 Sept
Puez-Odle high traverse, the all-dayer, or Alpe di Siusi with lunch at Gostner Schwaige if yesterday bit hard.
Mon 7 Sept
Via ferrata one: Sass Rigais with a Catores guide (€450 for the two of us, kit included) or self-guided Tridentina with rented kits (~€50). The bridge toward the course.
Tue 8 - Fri 11 Sept, version A
Cortina side: buses over on the 8th, Tre Cime and the Cadini spur on the 9th, the Lagazuoi tunnels (via ferrata two, headlamps on) with Cinque Torri and the Rifugio Averau long lunch on the 10th, travel to Arco on the 11th.
Tue 8 - Fri 11 Sept, version B
The rooms-only hut traverse below: bus to Passo Falzarego and a short day to Averau on the 8th, the long middle day to Staulanza on the 9th, the Civetta wall to Tissi on the 10th, out through Vazzoler and Carestiato to Passo Duran on the 11th and onward transport toward Arco that evening or the next morning.

How this was chosen, and the swaps: seven nights buys two bases at most (the west and east clusters sit 3.5 bus-hours apart), Val Gardena won the first base on car-free logistics, and the ferrata sits mid-week so exposure is familiar before the course. The geometry is a deliberate there-and-back: Cortina is the far corner, and the long Cortina-Arco transfer on the 11th (6-7 h by bus, ~3.5 h by car) is the price of the eastern icons; reversing the order saves almost nothing in total hours and moves the long haul onto jet-lag day, the single-base week avoids it by skipping the east, and the hut traverse walks the doubling-back on trails and exits pointing toward Arco. Everything here reorders freely against weather; only the Seceda slot, a guided ferrata and any hut nights pin dates. Named alternatives if the mood changes: Lago di Sorapis or Croda da Lago instead of the Cadini spur (better lakes, bigger crowds, both far easier with the car); the Adolf Munkel trail in Val di Funes, back on the menu if the car happens; Col dei Bos or Piz da Lech as a second proper ferrata; a single-base week in Val Gardena (cheapest, most weather-proof, loses Tre Cime and the tunnels but gains Sella and Sassolungo circuits and the Maximilian ferrata); or the northern hut line via Fanes, where the private huts have real rooms from ~€95.

Mid-morning bus Venice to Ljubljana (Nomago or FlixBus, 2-4 h, €12-30), pick up the hire car near the station in the afternoon.

The hut traverse

The alternative to the Cortina base: walk the central Alta Via 1 from Passo Falzarego to Passo Duran, the most scenic stretch and the easiest to book. Every hut on it is open through at least 20 September, and the profiles below come from their own sites and recent guest reports (July 2026 check).

Since we would rather have rooms than dorms, the line that matters is the rooms-only version: night one at Rifugio Averau (proper doubles with en suites, and the best kitchen on the route: an a la carte dinner reviewers compare to a restaurant, plus a real buffet breakfast), night two at Passo Staulanza (hotel-style twins with private bathrooms, unlimited hot water and a small sauna: the mid-trek reset), night three at Rifugio Tissi (a private room at €94, perched against the 1,000 m Civetta northwest wall with what is widely called the best sunset on the Alta Via 1). Days run roughly 2 h, 7 h, 5 h, then 6.5 h out via Vazzoler (coffee in its alpine garden) and Carestiato (the best lunch of the last day; their cakes are a guarded recipe) to Passo Duran. The short first day is a feature after the travel days, and the only dorm-shaped compromise left is that Tissi's mattresses sag and its showers are officially not guaranteed this dry year, so the proper wash happens at Staulanza.

The classic version, one notch cheaper and one notch dormier, sleeps Croda da Lago (lakeside at Lago Federa, 6-9 bed rooms, ordinary dinner but a genuinely good buffet breakfast), Coldai (big, busy, thin mattresses, but the terrace catches the Pelmo sunset and Lago Coldai at dawn is next door) and Tissi in a 4-bed room. Nuvolau, the 1883 summit-top hut with the famous sunrise, is dorm-only with one rationed €7 shower, so it fails the room test, magnificent as its position is.

Money note, now confirmed: our Austrian Alpine Club membership gets the CAI member rate at CAI-owned huts under the reciprocity agreement, roughly 20% off (Croda da Lago half board drops €73 to €58.50; Tissi's 4-bed room €80 to €64). The catch for the rooms-only line: Averau and Staulanza are privately owned and Tissi's private room carries no member discount, so privacy costs both the room premium and the discount. Bring the physical membership cards; at least one hut refuses digital ones. And carry cash through the Civetta stretch regardless.

Slovenia: ~19-24 September

Hire the car in Ljubljana, round trip: about €150-230 for the week at late-September rates, vignette included, and no cross-border paperwork since the whole loop stays in Slovenia. An Italian one-way was checked and loses badly (drop fees €150-500 where they exist at all). The menu, all within two hours of each other: Bled for the Ojstrica viewpoint and Vintgar gorge (€15, pre-book), Bohinj and the Vogel cable car, over the Vršič pass into the Soča valley (the free pass shuttle runs until 30 September) for a canyoning day (€60-70 each, operators verified running) and kayaks on the Soča, sport climbing at Osp and Mišja Peč on our fresh kits just as the karst season opens (the Osp cave stays 27-28° on hot days), Škocjan caves (€22, the one to pick over Postojna), and horses or hire bikes around Bled or Bohinj on the easy day. A Ljubljana Friday means the Odprta kuhna food market. Rooms book fine a few days out at that time of year.

The German split: ~24-30 September

Around the 24th we hand the car back and split. Fran: to Berlin, best via the Zagreb hop (bus 2-2.5 h, €10-20, then Ryanair to Berlin, ~€40-70, about 1.5 h in the air) since no direct Ljubljana-Berlin flight exists; a hub fare via Munich or Frankfurt is the comfort option if one shows under €150. Sam: the train to Munich (change at Villach, six hours, from €38) and on to Augsburg (30-45 minutes). Oktoberfest runs 19 September to 4 October, so Munich beds are silly money but Augsburg absorbs the spillover, the friends are there anyway, and a midweek Wiesn day works without reservations (weekday tents keep a quarter of their seats unreserved; a Maß is €14.90-15.90). Then the ICE to Berlin (about four hours, €20-40 booked ahead) around the 28th for a few Berlin days together with the various friends.

Greece: 30 September - 6/7 October

Eurowings flies Berlin to Kos daily at 09:00, landing 13:00, through our window (€80-180 booked ahead; the fallback is Berlin-Athens-Kos, which costs a whole day more). Taxi to Mastichari, the 30-minute ferry, and we are in Massouri by late afternoon on the 30th or the 1st, on the island for the festival on 2-4 October with our own ropes and a week of morning-shade climbing, midday swims and Telendos evenings around it. On the 5th, ferry and the hop to Athens for a night: the 08:00 Acropolis slot on the 6th if we want it, souvlaki at Kostas, then the flight home on Tuesday 6 or Wednesday 7 October. Both researched return builds land on those exact days: Emirates prices best on the Tuesday, and Scoot's Athens departure (the self-build with Singapore Airlines home from Changi) runs Wednesday morning at 08:55, which needs the Athens night before that we are taking anyway.

What gets booked now

In order: the Arco course emails (dates and the syllabus checklist, plus the own-gear question); the hut rooms if the traverse wins (Averau, Staulanza and Tissi take online bookings; the profiles and contacts are above); refundable Kalymnos rooms covering 30 September to 6 October before festival week fills; the Vienna leg for the 3rd (either the 18:20 Ryanair to Bergamo or the Vienna-Bolzano Sparschiene, both cheap enough to eat if plans flip); the Eurowings Berlin-Kos flight for 30 September or 1 October, which is the one date-locked cheap fare in the back half; the flight home from Athens for the 6th or 7th; and insurance the same week as the remaining flights. Small calls before flying: phone St Peters if we want to lead rather than top-rope on the Sydney morning, call BFF Tampines to flag the belay verification, and submit the Singapore arrival cards from Sydney.

Dates holding this together: Scoot's Vienna flight runs Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday (our 3 September is a Thursday); the Seceda lift and Tre Cime toll road both want pre-booked slots; the Vršič shuttle stops 30 September, one day after we leave Slovenia; Oktoberfest runs 19 September to 4 October with Augsburg as the sane base; Eurowings Berlin-Kos flies daily only until 18 October; the Kalymnos festival is 2-4 October; and the flight home is aimed at 6-7 October, where both return builds have their cheap days.

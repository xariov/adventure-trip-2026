Itinerary

Seceda and the Odle · photo: Wolfgang Moroder / CC BY-SA 3.0

Five weeks, and the route is set: Sydney, Singapore and Vienna to get there (booked, with a climbing gym at each stop), the Dolomites and Arco for the heart of it, then Slovenia, a German split, and Kalymnos for the finale, flying home from Athens on 6 or 7 October.

The order still follows the calendar. Early September is the Dolomites' best fortnight, Arco is good all month, Slovenia's karst crags open their season as we arrive with fresh kits, and Kalymnos improves right up to its festival. Germany sits where it does because that is where the friends are, and Oktoberfest happens to be running when Sam passes through Munich.

Getting there: 1-4 September, booked

Tue 1 Sept
Jetstar Brisbane to Sydney, night with the Dulwich Hill crew.
Wed 2 Sept, early
Sydney Indoor Climbing Gym St Peters, ten minutes from Dulwich Hill and on the way to the airport, opens at 06:00 on Wednesdays (the only day it opens that early). Climb 6:30-9:30 on top rope (walk-in after a ten-minute belay check; leading needs a pre-arranged test, 02 9519 3325), about $80 for two with full hire, then the 12-minute drive to T1 for the 13:00 Scoot departure. The no-time-pressure version is Tuesday evening instead, open to 22:00.
Wed 2 Sept, evening
Land Changi 19:30 with cabin bags only (the checked bags ride through on the single Scoot ticket; SG Arrival Cards submitted from Sydney). Fifteen minutes to BFF Climb at Tampines Hub: day pass S$23.98, shoes S$5.45, and their on-demand pair belay verification (S$10.90) puts us on real top ropes until about 22:30. Back airside by 23:15 for the ~02:45 Vienna flight. If we land late, the fallback is Climb@T3 in Terminal 3's public basement.
Thu 3 Sept
Land Vienna ~09:00 with the whole day in hand. Two branches, now shaped by the hire car: if the Milan friend confirms, a Vienna day then Ryanair at 18:20 to Bergamo and the evening in Milan, with the car picked up at Bergamo airport the next morning (big supply, cheap rates) for the ~4 h drive to Arabba. If Milan stays a maybe, the day train Vienna-Bolzano via Innsbruck (6.5-7.5 h, advance fares ~€35-45) and a Bolzano night, with the car picked up in Bolzano at 08:30 for the 1 h 50 drive up the Val Badia. The night-train option died with the timetable: Vienna-Milan no longer runs.
Fri 4 Sept
Pick up the car, drive to Arabba, check in (booked: 6 nights to the 10th). Recovery day by design: the Sass Pordoi cable car is ten minutes away if we want a zero-effort 2,950 m terrace, otherwise groceries, a short leg-stretch and an early night.

The Dolomites from Arabba: 5-10 September

Arabba is booked as the single base, and with the car it works as a hub: Corvara is 15-20 minutes over the Campolongo, Passo Gardena 35-40, the Falzarego trailheads about 35-40, Cortina d'Ampezzo around an hour, Tre Cime roughly 1 h 30, Ortisei's lifts about an hour, and the Marmolada is 25 minutes up the road (drive times approximate; the passes are slow-but-spectacular kilometres). No more base-swapping, no bus timetables, and days reorder freely against the forecast. The menu, one day each, pick five:

The icons day
Tre Cime circuit plus the Cadini spur: leave Arabba at dawn with a pre-booked toll slot (€40/car) and be walking by 8, ahead of the crowds; the drive back collects Misurina. The longest spoke of the week, and worth it.
The war-tunnels day
Park at Passo Falzarego: up the Kaiserjäger path, down through the Lagazuoi WWI tunnels (headlamps), then the Cinque Torri trenches and the long lunch at Rifugio Averau. The best single day in the range, 35 minutes from the door.
The Seceda day
Drive to Ortisei (~1 h), pre-booked lift slot, walk the ridgeline over the Odle. Or trade it for the Adolf Munkel trail in Val di Funes (~1 h 20), directly under the same walls, quieter and cheaper.
The ferrata day
Tridentina from Passo Gardena (30-35 min drive, kits rentable in Corvara or Selva) or Piz da Lech straight from Corvara's gondola; Sass Rigais with a Catores guide (€450 for two) stays the guided option. Col dei Bos at Falzarego can piggyback on the tunnels day if one ferrata isn't enough.
The doorstep day
What Arabba is for: Sass Pordoi cable car to 2,950 m and the walk to Piz Boè (3,152 m, the easiest three-thousander in the Dolomites), or the Viel del Pan balcony trail with the Marmolada glacier filling the horizon, twenty minutes from bed. Check the lift's late-season dates when booking the car.
The wildcard day
Weather's choice: Lago di Sorapis with a 07:00 start (the car makes that possible), Croda da Lago from Passo Giau, or Alpe di Siusi if a gentle day is owed (mind the 9:00-17:00 access-road closure; go via the Ortisei gondola).
Thu 10 Sept
Check out of Arabba. Either one last morning on a pass then the ~2 h drive south to drop the car in Trento (branches close for lunch, 12:30-14:00) and the local bus into Arco a day early, or an extra Dolomites night somewhere west (Val Gardena or Canazei) if the range has its hooks in; that night is currently unbooked either way.

The huts are scrapped: everything with rooms was booked out for our dates by the time we moved, which settles the old version A/B fork in favour of the car. The full hike-by-hike comparison (effort, difficulty, crowds, links) is on Destinations; with Arabba as the hub, the access column's car caveats all dissolve.

Arco: 11-17 September

Fri 11 - Sun 13 Sept
Settle in, the two-pass walk of the gear-shop street and the kit purchases (decided: we buy before the course), a warm-up top-rope session, a lake day at Riva or Torbole.
Mon 14 - Wed 16 Sept
The lead course with a UIAGM guide: clipping, lead belays, practice falls, threading anchors, bailing. On our own gear if we bought first. The whole trip funnels through these days.
Wed 16 - Thu 17 Sept
First fully independent leads at Massone and Nago on our own ropes. Celebration dinner at Alla Lega (closed Wednesdays, so the Thursday).
Fri 18 Sept
The Venice detour on the way out: train via Rovereto and Verona (~4.5 h, €20-30 each), bags into the station deposit, the cicchetti crawl (it is a weekday, so the classics are open), a night near the station.
Sat 19 Sept
Mid-morning bus Venice to Ljubljana (Nomago or FlixBus, 2-4 h, €12-30), pick up the hire car near the station in the afternoon.

Slovenia: ~19-24 September

Hire the car in Ljubljana, round trip: about €150-230 for the week at late-September rates, vignette included, and no cross-border paperwork since the whole loop stays in Slovenia. An Italian one-way was checked and loses badly (drop fees €150-500 where they exist at all). The menu, all within two hours of each other: Bled for the Ojstrica viewpoint and Vintgar gorge (€15, pre-book), Bohinj and the Vogel cable car, over the Vršič pass into the Soča valley (the free pass shuttle runs until 30 September) for a canyoning day (€60-70 each, operators verified running) and kayaks on the Soča, sport climbing at Osp and Mišja Peč on our fresh kits just as the karst season opens (the Osp cave stays 27-28° on hot days), Škocjan caves (€22, the one to pick over Postojna), and horses or hire bikes around Bled or Bohinj on the easy day. A Ljubljana Friday means the Odprta kuhna food market. Rooms book fine a few days out at that time of year.

The German split: ~24-30 September

Around the 24th we hand the car back and split. Fran: to Berlin, best via the Zagreb hop (bus 2-2.5 h, €10-20, then Ryanair to Berlin, ~€40-70, about 1.5 h in the air) since no direct Ljubljana-Berlin flight exists; a hub fare via Munich or Frankfurt is the comfort option if one shows under €150. Sam: the train to Munich (change at Villach, six hours, from €38) and on to Augsburg (30-45 minutes). Oktoberfest runs 19 September to 4 October, so Munich beds are silly money but Augsburg absorbs the spillover, the friends are there anyway, and a midweek Wiesn day works without reservations (weekday tents keep a quarter of their seats unreserved; a Maß is €14.90-15.90). Then the ICE to Berlin (about four hours, €20-40 booked ahead) around the 28th for a few Berlin days together with the various friends.

Greece: 30 September - 6/7 October

Eurowings flies Berlin to Kos daily at 09:00, landing 13:00, through our window (€80-180 booked ahead; the fallback is Berlin-Athens-Kos, which costs a whole day more). Taxi to Mastichari, the 30-minute ferry, and we are in Massouri by late afternoon on the 30th or the 1st, on the island for the festival on 2-4 October with our own ropes and a week of morning-shade climbing, midday swims and Telendos evenings around it. On the 5th, ferry and the hop to Athens for a night: the 08:00 Acropolis slot on the 6th if we want it, souvlaki at Kostas, then the flight home on Tuesday 6 or Wednesday 7 October. Both researched return builds land on those exact days: Emirates prices best on the Tuesday, and Scoot's Athens departure (the self-build with Singapore Airlines home from Changi) runs Wednesday morning at 08:55, which needs the Athens night before that we are taking anyway.

What gets booked now

In order: the hire car for the 4th (Bolzano or Bergamo pickup, following the Vienna choice, Trento drop); the Arco course emails when we feel like it (deferred by choice, since the on-request formats do not sell out; dates and the syllabus checklist are on the course page); refundable Kalymnos rooms covering 30 September to 6 October before festival week fills; the Vienna leg for the 3rd (either the 18:20 Ryanair to Bergamo or the Vienna-Bolzano Sparschiene, both cheap enough to eat if plans flip); the Eurowings Berlin-Kos flight for 30 September or 1 October, which is the one date-locked cheap fare in the back half; the flight home from Athens for the 6th or 7th; and insurance the same week as the remaining flights. Small calls before flying: phone St Peters if we want to lead rather than top-rope on the Sydney morning, call BFF Tampines to flag the belay verification, submit the Singapore arrival cards from Sydney, and install the eSIMs (a Bouygues Europe plan each plus a small Singapore one; details on Logistics).

Dates holding this together: Scoot's Vienna flight runs Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday (our 3 September is a Thursday); the Seceda lift and Tre Cime toll road both want pre-booked slots; the Vršič shuttle stops 30 September, one day after we leave Slovenia; Oktoberfest runs 19 September to 4 October with Augsburg as the sane base; Eurowings Berlin-Kos flies daily only until 18 October; the Kalymnos festival is 2-4 October; and the flight home is aimed at 6-7 October, where both return builds have their cheap days.

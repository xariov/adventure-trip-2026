The plan

Five weeks, and the way in is booked: Brisbane to Sydney on 1 September, a night with friends in Dulwich Hill (with a ropes session at a Sydney gym squeezed in), then Scoot from Sydney through Singapore (about seven hours there, ideally with a climb) to Vienna, landing on the 3rd. From Vienna we make our way to the Dolomites by the evening of the 4th, probably via a night with a friend in Milan. An easy day to shake off the flights, then hiking from the 5th: Seceda, Tre Cime, the Lagazuoi war tunnels, and one or two via ferrata to get comfortable on steep rock, with hut nights if the right huts have rooms. Arco from around the 11th for the lead course with a UIAGM guide, a kit each from the town's seventeen gear shops, and the first independent leads above Lake Garda.

Then the route runs north-east before it runs south: Slovenia from around the 18th for hiking, climbing at Osp, the Soča valley's canyons and maybe kayaks and horses, probably with a car. In the last week of September we split for Germany: Fran to Berlin for about a week, Sam to friends around Munich and Augsburg (Oktoberfest will be running) and then Berlin too. Around the 1st of October we fly from Berlin to Greece for the finale: Kalymnos on our own gear with the climbing festival on 2-4 October, an Athens day if it fits, and the flight home on 6 or 7 October.

What we said we wanted

The course comes first: the point is to fly home as lead climbers with a kit each, and any climbing after that is a bonus rather than an obligation. The first fortnight is planned and the rest is deliberately not; we would rather choose from a researched menu on the road than march through a schedule. Beyond that the trip leans outdoors: hiking, rock, sea swims and mountain food ahead of queueing for sights. We spend carefully by default and deliberately when something earns it, keep transit short relative to time on the ground, and want weather that's warm without being punishing.

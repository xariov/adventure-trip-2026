Slovenian flashcards

Enough Slovenian for the week of 19-24 September: greetings, the please-and-thank-you layer, asking the way, the place names, ordering, a room, the numbers, and the words that matter at a crag or on a trail. Every card carries a memory hook: a sound-alike or the root the word is built from. Tap a card to flip it, press Hint if you're stuck, mark it known or not, and the deck remembers on this device.

How to read the pronunciations

Capitals mark the stressed syllable. The sounds that trip up English speakers: c is always "ts" (as in cats), č is "ch", š is "sh", ž is "zh" (the s in measure), j is "y", h is a rough "h" (as in Scottish loch), r is rolled, and every letter is pronounced, so there are no silent vowels. An l or v at the end of a word or before a consonant softens to a "w" (bil sounds like "beew", v redu like "oo REH-doo"). Vowels are short and clean: a as in father, e as in bet, i as "ee", o as in sort, u as "oo".

Every card in this set is marked as known. Untick the filter or reset to go again.

Keyboard: ← → to move, space to flip, h hint, 1 not yet, 2 got it.

Help and emergencies

The emergency number is 112 across Slovenia and operators speak English. Mountain rescue is free for members of a partner alpine club and otherwise billed, which is one of the reasons the insurance on Logistics covers via ferrata and climbing.

Sources and conventions
  • Phrases follow standard Slovenian as spoken in Ljubljana and the Julian Alps. Coastal and Soča dialects vary in accent but the standard forms are understood everywhere.
  • Pronunciations are English-speaker approximations in the style of the Lonely Planet and Rick Steves phrasebooks, with stress marked in capitals. They are close rather than exact; the vowel lengths and pitch accent of native speech are not marked.
  • Emergency number 112 covers the whole country (Administration for Civil Protection and Disaster Relief); mountain rescue is the Gorska reševalna zveza Slovenije.

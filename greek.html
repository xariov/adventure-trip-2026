Greek flashcards

Enough Greek for Kalymnos and an Athens day: the alphabet, greetings, the please-and-thank-you layer, the ferry and the scooter, the taverna, the numbers, and the words that matter at the crag and in the water. Every card carries a memory hook. Tap a card to flip it, press Hint if you're stuck, mark it known or not, and the deck remembers on this device.

How to read the pronunciations

Capitals mark the stressed syllable, and Greek marks it too: the accent over a vowel is the stress, so read it off the word. Every vowel is short and clean, and there are only five sounds: a as in father, e as in bet, i as "ee" (spelled η, ι, υ, ει or οι, all the same sound), o as in sort, ou as "oo". The consonants that catch English speakers: δ is the th in this (written "th" here), θ is the th in thin, χ is a rough h (written "kh"), γ is a soft gargled g before a and o and a "y" before e and i, ρ is tapped. Two letters together change sound: μπ is b, ντ is d, γκ is g, τσ is ts. And the Greek question mark is a semicolon.

Every card in this set is marked as known. Untick the filter or reset to go again.

Keyboard: ← → to move, space to flip, h hint, 1 not yet, 2 got it.

Alphabet

Twenty-four letters, and you know half already from maths, physics and fraternity names. Nine look and sound like English. Nine more you know by name once the sound is attached. The whole job is the six traps that look like English letters and aren't, plus a handful of two-letter sounds. Capitals are easier than lower case, and the harbour signs are all capitals, so read those first.

Greetings

Help and emergencies

112 works across Greece and the operators speak English. Kalymnos has a health centre in Pothia and a helicopter to Rhodes or Kos for anything serious, which is why the insurance covers climbing.

Crag and sea

Belay calls at Kalymnos are English, and so are most of the climbers, but the rescue, the boat taxi and the weather board are Greek. And the sea is half the day.

Sources and conventions
  • Phrases are standard modern Greek as spoken in Athens and the Dodecanese. Kalymnos has its own accent and a few island words (moúri is one) but the standard forms are understood everywhere.
  • Pronunciations are English-speaker approximations in phrasebook style, with stress in capitals. "th" is the soft th of this unless the note says otherwise; "kh" is the rough h of Scottish loch.
  • Emergency numbers: 112 nationwide, 166 ambulance, 108 coast guard. The Kalymnos health centre is in Pothia.
